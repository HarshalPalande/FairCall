# Pitch video — two cuts: 5:00 max and 2:00

**Razorpay AI Buildathon, Track 02 — AI Risk Manager.**
Two scripts below: a full 5:00 cut, and a separate, independently-written 2:00
cut for a tighter slot — don't try to read the 5:00 script faster to fit 2:00,
use the 2:00 script instead, it's written for that pace on purpose.

Every number in both is reproducible with `make demo` / `make backtest`, or
live on the pages named. Re-verified this pass, not carried over stale.

## Before you record

```bash
make install && make demo && make backtest && make test    # warm everything
make app                                                    # Streamlit on :8501
```

Have open in tabs: terminal, `artifacts/backtest_comparison.png`, and these
Streamlit pages ready to jump to — **Four Way Simulator**, **Demo Mode**,
**RDR Optimizer**. Record at 1080p minimum.

**The one rule for both cuts:** every claim on camera should be visible as
output, not asserted over a slide. Don't narrate it — show it.

# THE 5:00 CUT

## 0:00 – 0:25 — The problem, stated once

**On screen:** title card, then straight to terminal.

> "A chargeback is a customer telling their bank to reverse a payment. Most
> merchants do one of two dumb things — accept everything, or contest
> everything. Both lose money, and both ignore that a chargeback has more
> than one lever: refund it before it's filed, deflect it, contest it, or
> send it to a human. We built the thing that prices all four — scoped to
> one reason code, Visa 13.1, 'merchandise not received.'"

## 0:25 – 0:50 — Honest framing, up front

**On screen:** the README's "Read this first" block.

> "One thing before any number: this data is synthetic, generated by a
> seeded script in this repo, not a repurposed fraud dataset wearing
> chargeback labels it was never measured against. Every number that
> follows measures the system design against a world we disclosed in full —
> it's not a benchmarked real-world result, and we don't claim it is."

**Why here, not at the end:** leading with the limitation turns your biggest
weakness into your clearest signal of judgment.

## 0:50 – 2:20 — The four-way engine, live and interactive

**On screen:** the **Four-Way Simulator** page, full screen. It opens already
on the disagreement — don't rebuild it, just point at what's already there.

> "This is the four-way engine, live — drag any of these and both engines
> re-run in real time. It opens on this: 69% win probability, full evidence,
> nothing blocked. The old two-output engine says contest — expected value
> plus ₹693, we'd probably win this fight."
>
> "The four-way engine still refunds it. Once you price in the ₹4,576 this
> merchant's portfolio-risk exposure costs on every filed dispute — win or
> lose — refunding beats winning by about ₹531. A two-output engine has no
> lever for 'give the money back on purpose.' This one does."

**Drag the win-probability slider down, live, to show it's not scripted:**

> "Drag win probability down — the moment it's no longer worth contesting
> either way, both engines agree. That convergence is the proof this isn't
> two different opinions, it's one system pricing a cost the other one
> can't see."

**Then the ceiling, the other non-negotiable case (switch to typing the
amount, or cut to the pre-built demo case if simpler):**

> "And here's the floor under all of it: raise the amount past ₹25,000 and
> every automated option — including this one — gets blocked, no matter how
> good the math looks. 64% win probability, EV of contesting is *plus*
> ₹11,239 — and it still escalates to a human, because that ceiling sits
> outside the EV calculation entirely. 99% confident doesn't override it.
> There's a unit test that asserts exactly that property, not a proxy for it."

## 2:20 – 3:00 — Demo Mode: the honest live batch

**On screen:** the **Demo Mode** page. Click "Run a new batch" live.

> "We could have built a demo that always catches the risky one. We didn't —
> here's why. This model scores transactions *before* any dispute exists,
> which is a genuinely harder problem than the reactive detector: no
> evidence exists yet at payment time. Its real recall is about 23%, and
> that's disclosed everywhere in this project, not hidden."
>
> "So instead of faking it, this page runs a fresh, unseeded batch of 100
> synthetic transactions every click — watch it happen live — and reports
> whatever actually occurs, misses included. What it does show, honestly:
> the easy cases, it nails almost every time. The disguised ones — that's
> the genuinely hard part of the problem, and you can see that gap right
> here, live, not asserted in a slide."

**If time allows, one line on the threshold slider:** "and this trade-off is
adjustable, live — catch more, at a real, honest cost in false alarms."

## 3:00 – 3:40 — The money, including the part that hurts

**On screen:** `artifacts/backtest_comparison.png`, then `make backtest`'s
KEY NUMBERS block.

> "Across 3,000 held-out disputes: accepting everything loses ₹6.68 million.
> Contesting everything nets ₹773,000. Our system nets ₹1.08 million — not
> from better win-rate prediction, but because 56% of disputes get resolved
> with zero analyst time, labor contest-everything spends regardless of
> whether a human's judgment was actually needed."

**Then, deliberately:**

> "The honest cost of automating that 56%: a 14.2% false-positive rate on
> the auto-contest path — ₹890,000, a headline number in the repo, not
> buried in an aggregate. If your system automates money decisions and you
> can't tell me its false-positive cost, you haven't finished."

**This is the highest-scoring 15 seconds in the video** — the rubric asks
for "honest metrics including false-positive cost" in those words.

## 3:40 – 4:15 — Governance you can watch working

**On screen:** `make tamper-demo`, live.

> "Every decision writes to an append-only, hash-chained audit log. Watch it
> actually work: I edit a past entry — turn a ₹42,000 escalation into a
> ₹9,000 auto-contest, exactly what covering a bad call looks like. Chain
> fails, names the line. I try to bury it by appending valid entries on top.
> Still fails — each entry hashes the previous hash plus its own body, so
> editing history breaks every link after it."

## 4:15 – 4:40 — Where the AI is, and why it's on a leash

**On screen:** `src/evidence_draft.py`.

> "The one place an LLM touches this system: drafting a dispute rebuttal
> letter. It never sees the raw case — only a fact ledger built from
> evidence actually on file. It can't write the letter, only emit claims
> citing a fact ID, and a pure-Python verifier rejects — doesn't repair,
> rejects — anything that cites a fact that doesn't exist. An approved
> draft is still just a draft. Nothing here submits anything anywhere."

## 4:40 – 5:00 — Close

**On screen:** `make test` → 124 passed.

> "124 tests, reproducible from a cold clone — install, demo, backtest,
> tests, and every number here comes from that one run. One reason code,
> four levers instead of one, done honestly, with the false-positive cost
> on the table. That's the submission."

# THE 2:00 CUT

## 0:00 – 0:20 — Problem + honesty, combined

> "A chargeback has four real responses, not two — refund it early, deflect

**On screen:** Four-Way Simulator, already open on the default disagreement.

> "69% win probability. Full evidence. The old two-output engine says
> contest — we'd probably win. This engine refunds anyway, because a filed
> dispute costs this merchant ₹4,576 in portfolio risk whether they win or
> lose it — win the fight, lose the portfolio. A two-output system has no
> way to say that. This one just did, live, not scripted."

## 1:00 – 1:35 — The honest number

**On screen:** `make backtest` KEY NUMBERS, or the pre-generated chart.

> "₹1.08 million net value across 3,000 disputes — not from smarter
> prediction, from not paying a human to review what didn't need one. And
> the cost of that automation, stated plainly: a 14.2% false-positive rate,
> ₹890,000. If a system automates money decisions and can't tell you its
> false-positive cost, it isn't finished. This one does."

## 1:35 – 2:00 — Close

> "Hash-chained audit log, an LLM that's only ever allowed to cite facts on
> file — never write past them — and 124 tests, reproducible from a cold
> clone. One reason code, four levers, done honestly. That's the pitch."

## Things not to do (both cuts)

- Don't read the architecture diagram aloud — it's in the README.
- Don't say "as you can see" — show the number instead.
- Don't apologize for synthetic data twice. Once, up front, framed as a
  decision, not an excuse.
- Don't claim Demo Mode "always catches it." It doesn't, on purpose, and
  the honest-miss framing is more convincing than a fake 100% would be.
- Don't demo every Streamlit page. The 5:00 cut visits three; the 2:00 cut
  visits one. That's deliberate, not a shortcut.
